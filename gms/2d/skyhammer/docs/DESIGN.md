# SKYHAMMER — DESIGN

Owned by DESIGN. Numbers here are the ones actually shipped in `js/data/*.js` — when this
document and the data disagree, the data is right and this file is stale; fix the file.

---

## 0. The shape of it

100 story levels across 5 acts of 20. Act 1 is hand-authored in full
(`js/data/levels.js`). Acts 2-5 are generated by `tools/gen_levels.mjs` from per-act
enemy pools into `js/data/levels_gen.js`, deterministic from a fixed seed, then
re-validated on every regeneration. Each act ends on a multi-part boss
(`js/data/enemies.js`, `boss_*` rows) worth roughly 2.5-3x a normal level's payout.

The tone: WW2 that gets less serious as it goes. Act 1 plays it straight. By Act 5 the
enemy doesn't have a flag, the player's rival ace has ejected from four different
aircraft and is somehow still flying, and there's an orbital laser on the shop shelf next
to the confetti bomb.

## 1. Act 1 — WW2 Europe (levels 1-20)

**One sentence:** the tutorial, dressed as a normal WW2 bombing campaign, because
teaching-by-doing reads better than a tutorial popup ever will.

Every mechanic in the game gets introduced here, once, cleanly, before Act 2 starts
mixing them:

| Levels | Teaches |
|---|---|
| 1 | Point-at-finger flight, main gun, basic `destroy` objective |
| 2 | Flak that shoots back, a second `destroy` objective in the same level |
| 3 | Enemy fighters (`kill` objective), landing on a carrier pad |
| 4 | Big/tough targets — nudges the player toward special-weapon bombs |
| 5 | Combining bunkers + fighters in one level |
| 6 | Balloons as a pure `collect` objective — no combat, so it reads clearly |
| 7 | Fighter-only level, 8 kills, dogfighting under pressure |
| 8 | Radar as a target type, night lighting |
| 9 | Desert biome, moving armour (`tank.moves`) |
| 10 | Alpine biome, mixed radar + bunker |
| 11 | City biome, flak + fighters together |
| 12 | Three objective types in one level (destroy + kill + collect) |
| 13 | Storm weather, a `survive` objective |
| 14 | A named heavy bomber as a "mini-boss" fighter, escort tactics |
| 15 | Second landing, different pad (`airstrip` vs `carrier`) |
| 16 | Full combined-arms level, night, longest length so far |
| 17 | Largest pre-boss level: three objective types, five enemy categories |
| 18 | Recon/breather level — mostly collection, sets up the boss narratively |
| 19 | Gauntlet — heavy flak + large fighter wave, tension before the fight |
| 20 | **Boss: The Iron Duke** |

**Boss — The Iron Duke** (`boss_ironduke`): an armoured gunship zeppelin. Two engine
nacelles, two turrets, an armoured gondola core. Teaches the multi-part-boss pattern
(kill the guns to stop taking damage, then the core) at the lowest possible stakes —
lowest boss HP pool of the five (1320 total across parts), cheapest boss payout (1800).

Biomes used: farmland, sea, city, desert, alpine, coast (all six). Weather: clear,
overcast, storm. Time of day: dawn, day, dusk, night. Every combination ART needs to
have covered by the time a player reaches level 20 is listed in `DESIGN_NOTES.md`.

## 2. Act 2 — Wider War (levels 21-40)

**One sentence:** same war, same enemy, but the raids get bigger and the toys get better
— this is "more," not "different," so the player's new plane tiers (Harrow → Tempest)
and new specials (heavy bomb, cluster, rockets) get a real workout before the setting
moves on.

Introduces: `halftrack`/`railyard`/`uboat` ground targets, `fw190`/`he111` aircraft,
`balloon_gold` (rarer, pays more). Enemy counts per level scale up roughly linearly with
position-in-act (see `tools/gen_levels.mjs` `groundCount`/`flakCount`/`fighterCount`
formulas). No new mechanic — this act is about *scale and mastery*, the "second lesson"
every long campaign needs before it changes the rules again.

**Boss — Leviathan** (`boss_leviathan`): a super-battleship. Bow gun, stern gun, two
midships guns, an armoured bridge core. Bigger HP pool than the Duke, still WW2-flavoured,
still fair — this is the "you've got this" boss that confirms the player's Act 1 plane
upgrades were worth it.

## 3. Act 3 — The Drift (levels 41-60)

**One sentence:** the war quietly stops being WW2 — prototype jets, SAM batteries and an
ace flying something that shouldn't exist yet turn up in what is still nominally 1944,
because the brief says the game "does not take itself seriously" and the *seams* of that
are funnier than committing to it from level 1.

Introduces: `sam_site` (a flak type that actually threatens altitude), `comms_tower`,
`convoy_truck`, and the first jets — `proto_jet`, `mig_ghost` — flying alongside legacy
`bf109`/`fw190` squadrons. The player's own ladder crosses into jets here too (`meteor`
at tier 4, `sabre` at tier 5), so plane and enemy tech drift together. Story beats
(`js/data/story.js`) lean into the anachronism explicitly rather than hand-waving it.

**Boss — Black Sigma** (`boss_blacksigma`): an experimental prototype bomber-fighter
hybrid. Two engine pods, a chin gun (reuses the `sam_site` firing profile), an armoured
cockpit core with the highest single-part HP yet (900) — this is the first boss where
"just shoot the core" stops being fast enough and the player needs to actually clear the
pods first.

---

## 4. Act 4 — Jet Age (levels 61-80)

**One sentence:** full commitment to supersonic dogfighting and a proxy-war map — this
act is the mechanical peak of "realistic-ish" combat before Act 5 throws that away.

Introduces: `reactor` (high-value/high-HP ground target), `laser_turret`,
`aa_carrier` (mobile heavy flak), `jet_fighter`, `stealth_drone` (fast, erratic, low HP —
a dodge check rather than a tank check). Player ladder: `vampire` (tier 6), `phantom`/
Revenant (tier 7). The bunker buster and (by the end of the act) the tactical nuke both
sit comfortably in the player's price range here.

**Boss — Behemoth** (`boss_behemoth`): a four-engine flying fortress. Four separate
engine parts plus a top turret plus an armoured hull core — the widest boss so far
(600 world units), designed to force the player to actually use the play area instead of
sitting on one side.

---

## 5. Act 5 — Chrome Sky (levels 81-100)

**One sentence:** the "faintly cyberpunk" landing zone the brief asked for — drone
swarms, mech walkers and a corporate-flavoured final boss, closing the "who are we even
fighting" joke that Act 3 opened.

Introduces: `drone_hive`, `mech_walker`, `plasma_nest`, `cyber_interceptor`, and
`drone_swarm` (cheap, fast, spawned in packs — the game's first real swarm enemy).
Player ladder tops out here: `specter` (tier 8, stealth, no visible engine, nobody in
the squadron will explain it) and `vector` (tier 9, `plasma` main gun, the "future" era
plane). The Orbital Strike special (tier 6, priced at 42,000, above the nuke) belongs to
this act specifically — it's the "final toy," bigger than the nuke in price and blast
radius, and it's the joke payoff for `nuke`'s "you survive your own blast" rule: even the
literal orbital laser strike is survivable, because friendly blast damage is permanently
zero (`tuning.js COMBAT.friendlyBlast = 0.0`).

**Boss — ORBITAL MOTHER** (`boss_orbitalmother`): a corporate orbital platform. Three
turret nodes, a regenerating-flavoured shield part (`tag:'shield'`, no `weak` flag — it
has to be worn down, not sniped), and the highest-HP core in the game (2200). Final boss,
final payout (24,000 base before act-boss reward on top), and the last story beat is the
rival ace still, somehow, ejecting safely.

## 6. Difficulty curve

Difficulty rises on three independent axes so no single stat carries the whole curve:

1. **Enemy count** — `groundCount`/`flakCount`/`fighterCount` in `gen_levels.mjs` scale
   with both position-in-act (`p`) and act number, so act 2 level 1 is already busier
   than act 1 level 19.
2. **Enemy quality** — each act's pool swaps to strictly tougher rows (see the tables in
   `enemies.js`'s act-grouped comments): hp, gun tier and turn rate all step up per act,
   not just per level.
3. **Player power** — the plane and weapon ladders (`planes.js`, `weapons.js`) are paced
   against the same 100 levels via `economy.js`'s two curves (§7 below), so the player's
   ceiling rises just ahead of the content's floor, never behind it.

`par` (the 2/3-star time threshold) grows roughly with `length` but not linearly with
level number — busier levels get proportionally more par time per objective, not just per
world-unit, which is why `tools/gen_levels.mjs`'s par formula includes an explicit
`(groundCount + flakCount + fighterCount) * 2.5` term on top of the length-based base.

## 7. Money & XP curve

Full maths, the two reference curves (competent vs scraper player) and the affordability
table live in `js/data/economy.js` as a comment block next to the code that computes it —
that file is the source of truth, this is the summary:

- Reward money per level rises from 250 (level 1) to 10,500 (level 99, act 5's next-to-
  boss level), with each act's boss paying roughly 2.5-3x that act's top non-boss reward.
- Two curves were checked against the plane price ladder: a **competent** player (mostly
  3-stars, ~1.2x base reward) and a **scraper** (mostly 1/2-stars, ~0.72x base). Both
  land inside "next plane tier every 8-12 levels" once you account for the gap being an
  upper bound that assumes zero spend on upgrades/specials along the way — see the table
  in `economy.js` for the exact per-tier level numbers.
- The floor rule — "always afford *something* after a level" — holds structurally: the
  worst-case single reward (level 1, scraper multiplier) still clears the cheapest
  upgrade tier's base price.

## 8. Weapons & planes

`js/data/weapons.js` ships the 8 "spirit of CONTRACTS §7" specials (bomb, heavy bomb,
cluster, rockets, seeker, firebomb, bunker buster, nuke) plus 6 original fun weapons
(party bomb, chicken bomb, boomerbomb, kraken torpedo, disco ball EMP, orbital strike),
priced across tiers 1-6 to slot into the same escalation. `js/data/planes.js` ships a
9-tier plane ladder (up from the original 6) specifically so "next tier every 8-12
levels" is achievable with only ~2 tiers per act rather than one every 33 levels.

## 9. Modes (`js/data/modes.js`)

- **Survival** — one arena, endless waves, 7 escalating tiers cycling the same act enemy
  pools `gen_levels.mjs` uses, then an open-ended overflow multiplier once the player
  outlasts tier 7.
- **Time Attack** — replay any beaten story level against `par`-derived gold/silver
  thresholds, ghost-replay flagged as expected but left to ENGINE/UI to implement.
- **Boss Rush** — all five bosses back to back, refuelled but only healed to 50% between
  fights, one lump-sum payout plus partial credit per boss downed.
- **Weekly Special Event** — a 7-entry rotation picked deterministically from the ISO
  week number (`getWeeklyEvent(date)`), no server required, same event for everyone in a
  given week.
