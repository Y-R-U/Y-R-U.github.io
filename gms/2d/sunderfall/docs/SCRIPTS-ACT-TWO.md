# SUNDERFALL — act two, every spoken line and the takes to generate

Everything said in the last three quarters of the game, plus the three Suno prompts that record
it. This file is complete on its own: nothing here needs cross-referencing to
`VOICE-AND-MUSIC.md` to generate a take. That file still owns the intro, the music, and the
character notes on the two original voices.

The lines are copied from `game/js/story/scenes.js` and `game/js/sim/barks.js`, which are the
source of truth. **If a recording comes back saying something slightly different, change the
game to match the recording** — a bubble that disagrees with the voice reads as a bug. That has
already happened four times in this project (VOICE-AND-MUSIC §3).

---

## 1. What act two is

Ostrick tells Rook to keep the fire lit, and then the fire goes out. That is the whole plot.

At the standing stones a second adult in a row hands him the job and walks away — Vayne did it
dying, Ostrick does it with a plan, which is worse. Rook holds the brazier through a defence
encounter and it is snuffed anyway, drunk from the inside by something behind the rock face that
has been leaning on it since the intro. The breach opens east onto a scorched track, and the
track climbs back to the Glyphglade: the clearing where Vayne died, with the ward circle still
burnt into the ground and the staff still standing where he fell. There the Seam speaks. It has
no voice of its own so it uses the last one it heard, and it gets Vayne wrong in ways that are
specific and cheap — too even, no tremble, it repeats itself, and it calls him *Rook*, which
Vayne never did. He was "boy" for the whole of the intro. Rook works out what he is listening to,
takes the staff because Vayne held it, and walks east into the arena. The way closes behind him.

He wins the way Vayne won: by spending what he has. He survives it because the old man already
paid. Ostrick arrives afterwards with three elders, far too late, and offers the only apology a
functionary has — paperwork. Rook says no and goes home to feed the goats.

**Four scenes, three new recordings.** Scene 2 (the stones) is already written and approved;
scenes 3, 4 and 5 are new. Every line is silent in the game until the three takes below exist.

### 2d. `after` — victory, the arena, ~54s

The seam is closed and the arena is rubble. **Hold the silence longer than is comfortable** —
six seconds before anyone says anything. Rook is still standing, just. The lifestone is spent and
dim.

Ostrick arrives from the west with three elders, out of breath, far too late. He does not
apologise. He starts a *procedural* question, which is exactly who he is, and hears himself
partway through it. **The elders say nothing at all, and go on saying nothing** — that is more
damning than a speech, and it is the reason not one of them has a line.

Rook's "No." is the flat single word Ostrick refused him with at the stones, handed back.

| — | *(silence. The elders do not speak. They never do.)* | |

| **Ostrick** | If you want it. | |

| — | *(he walks west. Home.)* | |
| **Ostrick** | …Boy. | Calling after him. Rook does not turn round. |

## 3. The three takes

House format: the blockquote is Suno's **style** box, the fenced block is the **lyrics** box.
Bracketed lines are performance direction, which Suno reads as instruction rather than as text.

Generate several of each and reject on tone before anything else. The failure modes are
predictable and named under each.

### 3a. `game/audio/vo/ostrick.mp3` — Keeper Ostrick, one take

Both his scenes in one recording: the stones first, then the four lines from the ending. He is a
**functionary**, not a mentor and not a villain. Sixties, provincial, clipped and pedantic, faster
than Vayne and harder than Rook. Forty years of rites and service, and Vayne turned him down and
melded a farm boy in a panic — his outrage is *procedural*, not moral, which is what makes it
funny before it turns frightening.

> **Style:** spoken word, no singing, older male voice, clipped and irritable and pedantic,
> provincial rather than grand, close-mic'd and dry, no reverb, almost no music, one low sustained
> drone and a faint crackling fire, no drums, no melody, D minor

```
[spoken, older man, clipped, irritable, pedantic]
Don't touch the stones.

Who melded you?

[flat, refusing it]
No.

[reciting regulations, offended]
There's a Rite. There's a Naming. There's nine years of it.
You got a dying man's panic.

[bitter, the real wound]
Vayne turned down forty years of me.
And gave it to what was standing there.

[thrown, slower]
You'd give it up.

[quiet, frightened now]
Then he was out of time.
He'd never. Not unless there was none left.

[to himself]
The seam's open.

[sharp, decided, already leaving]
Boy. Keep the fire lit.
Nothing crosses the stones while it burns.
I'll bring the elders.

[over his shoulder, not kind]
Learn something while you wait.

[out of breath, having run, much older suddenly]
Boy.
What rite did you—

[quiet, conceding]
Yes.

[offering the only thing he has]
There's a Rite. There's a Naming.
If you want it.

[carefully, no authority left in it]
Then what will you do?

[calling after him, not expecting an answer]
Boy.
```

Suno's defaults for an old man with a staff are **wise mentor** and **sneering villain**. Reject
both. The target is *petty* — a man complaining about paperwork — right up until "Then he was out
of time", where it should go quiet rather than dramatic. The last five lines want the same voice
with the fight gone out of it; if a take gives you a warm, kindly old man there, it is wrong —
he is embarrassed, not tender.

The three ellipsis lines in the game (`…Yes.`, `…Then what will you do?`, `…Boy.`) are written
without the leading dots here on purpose. The dots are the pause *before* the line, and they are
built into the beat timings; do not ask Suno to perform them.

### 3b. `game/audio/vo/rook2.mp3` — Rook, take two

Every new Rook line in the game — all four scenes, in scene order — followed by the nineteen new
barks from `VOICE-AND-MUSIC.md` §8e.

> ⚠ **This must be generated as a CONTINUATION of `game/audio/vo/rook.mp3`, not as a fresh
> generation.** A new generation will not be the same boy, and the game cuts between the intro's
> take, the existing `barks.mp3`, and this one within minutes of each other. Continuation is the
> only thing that held the voice together the first time — it is why `barks.mp3` sounds like the
> cinematic.
>
> The method, from `docs/VO-TIMING-RECIPE.md`: **start the extension a few seconds inside the
> previous take** so the model has its own voice in front of it to copy, then trim that lead-in
> off the front of the result and **subtract the trim from every offset you measure**. The trim
> was 12% of the file last time. Step 6 of the recipe (round-tripping every cut back through
> whisper) is what proves the subtraction was exact — do it.

> **Style:** spoken word, no singing, teenage male voice, flat and deadpan and unimpressed, dry
> close narration, almost no music, faint wind and distant crickets, D minor

```
[spoken, teenage boy, flat, deadpan, unimpressed]
Elderman Vayne.

Yes.

I had goats. Now I've got this.
Take it. Just take it.

Yes.

How long?

[flat, to nobody]
That's the second one.

[quieter, almost to himself]
The goats were fine.

[watching something wrong happen, still bored]
That's not the wind.
It's bending in.
Something's drinking it.

[flat, to nobody]
He said keep it lit.
Fine. I can do fire.

[it did not work]
It won't take.
That's never happened.

Something's been leaning on that.

[out of options, not brave]
I'm not standing here in the dark.
East, then.

[quiet, recognising a place]
I know these stones.
The circle's still burnt in.
This is where he did it.

[kneeling, small, to a dead man]
You left this standing.
The fire went out.

[not hope, barely a question]
Vayne.

[counting, quiet]
He only said that once.
He never called me that. I was boy.

[awake now, not raised]
You've got his voice. You haven't got him.

[level, one word]
Stop.

He held this.
It won't help. I'm taking it anyway.
You don't get to keep him.

[exhausted, flat, after everything]
Is that it?
The stone's gone out.

It's done.

[flat, final, the same word he was refused with]
No.

[the last line, deadpan, no warmth and no joke]
Nobody's fed the goats.

[muttering, to himself, walking]
Yep. Still flammable.
Every time. Every single time.

Fine. That's fine.
I've had worse. Recently.

[strained, hurt]
Not here. Not for this.
Keep the fire lit, he said.

[deadpan]
Sorry. To whoever built that.
That was somebody's wall.

[uneasy]
That's the stone. Not me.
I don't like how easy that was.

It fits better now. That's worse.
Bigger. Great.

Down again. Fine.
Walls. Use the walls.

I know what to do with rock now.

[quiet, to nobody, a long walk]
Nobody ever needed saving from a goat.
Cass would hate this. Small mercy.
I'd take the goats.
Keep the fire lit.
```

Reject anything cute, wry or knowing — **bored** is still the target, and the new lines are
*tired* rather than sad. Three specific traps:

- **"Stop."** will come back shouted. It must not be. It is the loudest thing in the game and it
  is said at conversational volume.
- **"Nobody's fed the goats."** is the last line of the game. If the take winks at it, the ending
  is gone. It should sound like a chore he has remembered.
- **"The goats were fine"** and **"I'd take the goats"** are the only two lines in the game with
  any warmth in them. Warm, not fond. Not a punchline.

Note `I was boy` in the lyrics: the game's text is `I was 'boy'.` with quotes, which Suno will
either read aloud or choke on. Generate it bare and leave the quotes in the game — they are
punctuation for the eye, and the ear gets them from the emphasis.

### 3c. `game/audio/vo/vayne2.mp3` — the Seam, wearing Vayne

Six lines. This is the hardest ask in the file and everything about it is a trap.

> ⚠ **A CONTINUATION of `game/audio/vo/vayne.mp3`.** That is the entire effect: the Seam is not
> *doing an impression* of Vayne, it is using the recording of him. If this is generated fresh it
> is a different old man and the scene is nothing. Same method as 3b — start a few seconds inside
> `vayne.mp3`, trim the lead-in, subtract it from every offset.

**And then performed wrong.** Vayne's own take is gravelled, breathless, strained, unsteady,
with audible breath between phrases. This one is the same voice with all of that removed: no
tremble, no breath, no strain, no rise or fall. Each sentence at exactly the same pitch and
exactly the same pace as the one before it, with an identical gap between them. **It must sound
like a kind old man reading from a card.**

> **Style:** spoken word, no singing, elderly male voice, completely flat and even, no emotion,
> no breath, unhurried and mechanical in pacing, every sentence the same pitch and the same
> length, warm timbre but no feeling in it, close-mic'd and dry, no reverb, no processing, no
> effects on the voice, near silence underneath — one sustained low drone, no drums, no melody,
> D minor

```
[spoken, elderly man, flat, even, no breath, no tremble, reading aloud from a card]
You're what's here.

You're what's here.

That's the whole of it.

Rook.

Grow up. Quickly.
```

**Suno's instinct here is a demon voice, and it will ruin the scene.** Reject, without listening
twice, anything that arrives:

- pitched down, doubled, layered, whispered, growled, or with a second voice underneath
- with reverb, echo, or any "otherworldly" processing
- menacing, sneering, or knowing — **the Seam is not evil and it is not taunting him.** It does
  not know it is doing an impression. It is repeating the last thing it heard because that is all
  it has.
- warm or kindly in a way that *acts* — no gentleness, no sympathy, no pity. Flat.

If a take is too expressive but otherwise right, the fix that has worked is asking again with
`[monotone]` and `[no emotion, no inflection, robotic pacing but a human voice]` and nothing else
in the bracket.

**On the three "You're what's here."** — the line appears three times in the scene. Prefer to cut
**one** slice and point all three beats at it. An identical waveform played three times is not a
shortcut, it is the correct reading: the thing is replaying a recording, and a human ear catches
sameness at that resolution instantly. The lyrics ask for two so that there is a spare if the
first read is poor.

`Rook.` is the single most important word in the take and it is one syllable, so it is the one
most likely to be swallowed or merged into the line before it. Check it survived the generation
before spending time cutting anything else, and check it holds the same flatness — if it lands
with any tenderness at all the scene inverts and becomes a ghost story.

## 4. Once the files exist

1. **Encode and drop them in `game/audio/vo/`** beside `vayne.mp3`, `rook.mp3` and `barks.mp3`,
   named exactly `ostrick.mp3`, `rook2.mp3`, `vayne2.mp3` — `story/script.js`'s `TAKES` map
   already points at those paths.

   ```bash
   ffmpeg -i in.mp3 -map 0:a -ac 1 -ar 32000 -c:a libmp3lame -q:a 8 out.mp3
   ```

   `-map 0:a` is not optional: Suno embeds cover art and without it ffmpeg processes the picture
   stream and reports zero samples. Re-encoding does not shift the timeline, so timings may be
   measured on either file.

2. **Cut the lines** by the method in `docs/VO-TIMING-RECIPE.md` — whisper for identity and rough
   position, the envelope for local checks, the 11–22 cps sanity rule to catch bad boundaries, and
   **never let either one decide alone.** Remember the continuation trim: subtract it from every
   offset in `rook2` and `vayne2`.

3. **Paste the offsets in.** Each scene in `game/js/story/scenes.js` has a
   `── VO: <take> ──` comment at the top of its beats marking the block; every line's `vo: null`
   becomes `vo: [offset, length]`, seconds into that **file**, not into the scene. The new bark
   lines live in `game/js/sim/barks.js` and take their offsets the same way. Change nothing else —
   not a `t`, not a `dur`. If a line genuinely will not fit its beat, move the beat here and say so.

4. **The round-trip check in `docs/VO-TIMING-RECIPE.md` step 6 is not optional.** Cut each clip
   out, transcribe *that clip alone*, and compare the first word, the last word and the word count
   against the line you meant to cut. It is the only thing standing between the game and a bubble
   that plays half a sentence — it is how `blocked[1]` was caught being a fragment. Do it against
   the shipped file, after trimming and encoding, and it doubles as proof that neither operation
   moved the timeline.

5. **Run the checker**, which will not catch a bad cut but will catch a scene that no longer adds
   up:

   ```bash
   node tools/checkscenes.mjs
   ```

6. **Play all four scenes with sound on**, in portrait and in landscape. A line that reads fine
   silently can still be a second longer than its bubble.

Until every one of a take's lines is cut, the lines that are cut still play and the rest are
silent — a scene never waits on audio. The barks are stricter: **a bark whose take is not on disk
is not selectable at all**, because one silent line in a pool of voiced ones reads as a bug rather
than as restraint. That is why the nineteen new barks do nothing until `rook2.mp3` lands.
