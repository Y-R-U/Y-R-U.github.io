# 1. The world

## 1.1 The premise

A war that both governments have stopped being able to end.

Twelve years ago the **Verrine Republic** and the **Kohlgard Estates** fought over a river plain
called **the Sallow** and broke the dykes doing it. The Sallow is now three hundred kilometres of
standing water, drowned villages, and the tops of poplars. Nothing crosses it. The railways are
under it. The roads are under it. The **Kettle Sea** to the north was mined by both sides in the
same fortnight and neither kept a chart.

So everything that keeps anyone alive arrives **from above**.

Eight neutral freight houses out of the free port of **Vantry** run the **Concord Line**: enormous
grey cargo airships that cross the Sallow at four thousand metres, above the flak, above the
weather, on a published timetable, and push crates out of a hatch under parachutes. They do not
land. They do not aim. They have never once come lower to look.

A crate takes about ninety seconds to come down. In those ninety seconds it belongs to nobody.

**That is the game.** Get under it and take it home, or put a burst through the canopy so it falls
short of the field it was drifting towards. Flour, quinine, mail, magnetos, aviation spirit,
letters. Whoever's aerodrome it lands on, keeps it. Everyone on both sides calls it **the Fall**.

## 1.2 Why it gets worse

The uncomfortable part is not that people fight over food. It is *who is dropping it.*

- **Act 1** — the crates are supplies and the Fall feels like a sport. Everyone's grandfather flew
  post; this is post with a fight attached. It is genuinely fun, and it is meant to be.
- **Act 2** — the crates start arriving *on a schedule*. Eleven o'clock, four o'clock, every day,
  in weather that grounds everyone. The Line is not responding to need. It is **maintaining
  parity** — a drop over Verrine ground, then a drop over Kohlgard ground, and the two air arms
  wear each other out at a rate that keeps both accounts open.
- **Act 3** — the crates get heavier and the stencils change, and one of them breaks on landing at
  Ferrow Green and is full of **children**. The Line has been selling evacuation out of the Sallow
  by the pannier, at four hundred marks a head, dropped blind through cloud with everything else.
  A Kohlgard pilot has spent eight months shooting those canopies without ever opening one.
- **Act 4** — the Verrine Air Board orders the squadron to shoot canopies **over its own towns**,
  because a crate that lands in a Verrine village is a crate the Republic did not get. Both sides
  are now fighting over goods their own treasuries have already paid for. The player is ordered to
  attack a neutral airship.
- **Act 5** — the armistice was signed nineteen days ago. Nobody stopped, because the Line's
  contracts run to the end of the quarter and there is no clause for peace. The war is being
  carried, in the air, by an invoice.

The last drop of the quarter is the last hostile act of a war that ended three weeks earlier. The
game ends with someone taking that manifest and giving it away.

## 1.3 The Ladder — the map is vertical

Everyone in this world names the sky in layers, because the sky is the terrain. The player learns
these words in Act 1 and they are used in every briefing after it.

| layer | height | what is there |
|---|---|---|
| **the Mud** | 0–200 m | standing water, poplar tops, church spires, the drowned villages. Ground fire, and no room to recover. |
| **the Belt** | 200–900 m | flak. Both sides shoot into it and neither side is choosy. |
| **the Floor** | 900–2 000 m | working height. Most of the fighting. Where a crate is caught. |
| **the Deck** | 2 000–3 500 m | the cloud deck. You go through it blind; things wait on top of it. |
| **the Lane** | 3 500–4 500 m | the Concord Line's altitude. Cargo airships and their escorts. |
| **the Blue** | 4 500 m + | thin air. The engine leans out, the guns freeze, you can see the Kettle Sea and the sun at once. Nobody can stay. |

Agent B: these six names are the story's contract with the altitude bands. If the band edges move,
the names should move with them; the *words* should survive, because every briefing uses them.

## 1.4 Places worth naming

**Verrine (the player's side — "the Greys").** A tired republic of canal towns run by committee.
Dove-grey and ochre. Its air arm is the **Republic Air Service**; the player is **B Flight, 4th
Squadron**, and everyone calls them *the Ferrow Green lot*.

- **Ferrow Green** — home. A requisitioned racecourse. The hangar is the grandstand, the wireless
  room is the judge's box, and the fuel is stacked where the bookmakers stood. There is still a
  painted board reading **RESULTS** that nobody has taken down.
- **Weir, Hoy, Marrender, Coyle, Ashbourn** — the drowned villages of the Sallow. Nell flew the
  post between them. Six of the nine are under water; three are half up.
- **Tallowmoor** — the Verrine town that Act 4 orders the flight to deny crates to.

**Kohlgard (the enemy — "the Brass").** A federation of hunting estates and iron towns east of the
Sallow, aristocratic, courteous, and comprehensively out of money. Oxblood and brass. Its pilots
buy their own aeroplanes and paint them how they like, which is why the aces are recognisable at
two miles.

- **Marnhault** — the capital. Level 64 flies over it at dawn and it is the most beautiful thing in
  the game: white terraces, black river, a thousand chimneys and one enormous unfinished cathedral.
- **Hallowmere** — the Kohlgard aerodrome. It is the Countess's lawn.

**The Concord Line (neutral, and the antagonist).** Eight freight houses, one timetable. Their
airships are named for virtues, which is the joke: ***Patience***, ***Forbearance***,
***Prudence***, ***Restraint***, and the flagship, the ***Sufficiency***. Every crate is stencilled
with the Line's mark — a circle with a bar through it — which every pilot on both sides calls
**the noose**.

- **Vantry** — the free port on the Kettle Sea. Never visited. Referred to constantly, the way
  people refer to a head office.

# 2. The player

**Nell Corrigan**, twenty-six. Before the war she flew the **Sallow post** — mail, a doctor
sometimes, a coffin once — between nine villages in an aeroplane with no guns and a letterbox
bolted to the fuselage. She was not a fighter pilot and is not one now. She is a *delivery* pilot,
which is why she is extraordinarily good at the only thing this war rewards: being in exactly the
right piece of air at exactly the right second, in weather nobody else will fly.

She flies because the post route is her entire map of the world and it is under water, and crates
are the only cargo left to carry. She is not avenging anything. That is deliberate.

**Where she ends up.** Not on a side. Act 5 hands her the *Sufficiency*'s last manifest — the final
quarter's drops, already paid for by two treasuries at war with each other on paper only — and she
drops it on the towns instead of the fields. The epilogue is level 100: her machine repainted post
ochre, the guns unshipped and left on the grass at Ferrow Green, one bag of mail, and the first
delivery to Marrender in twelve years. She ends the game as the postmistress of a country that has
not been drawn yet.

> **PC variant.** The manifest supports a male alternate — **Nils Corrigan**, same age, same
> history, same lines — as one field in the save (`pc.voice = 'f' | 'm'`) and one voice id swap.
> No line of dialogue changes; nobody addresses Nell by a gendered word anywhere in §6. That is a
> constraint on the script and I have held to it. Default is Nell.

# 3. The cast

Everyone here has one want, stated in one sentence, and it is the thing that makes their lines
writeable. `voice` keys map to the table in `SUNO.md` §3.

## 3.1 Ferrow Green

**Marla Hurdle — "Hurdy"** · 22 · wireless operator · `hurdy`
The most-heard voice in the game by a wide margin: every briefing, most in-flight calls, most
debriefs. Dry, quick, permanently three jobs behind. She works the judge's box with a paraffin
stove and a logbook. She reads the recovered mail out over the wire because there is nobody else to
do it and it is the only good part of the day.
**Wants:** to hear from her brother, who was a sapper on the Sallow dykes and has been *missing,
believed* since before the flood. She reads every letter out of every crate. She never says why.

**Tobin Ferris — "Ferry"** · 60 · fitter · `ferry`
Was the racecourse farrier before it was an aerodrome. Now he bolts salvage to aeroplanes.
Deadpan, unhurried, hoards. **He is the hangar and upgrade voice**, so he speaks more than anyone
except Hurdy — write him a deep pool.
**Wants:** one aeroplane still flying on the last day of the war, whole, so there is something to
put in a museum. He will tell you this once, in Act 1, and never mention it again until Act 5.

## 3.2 B Flight

**Ruthven Halke — "Roo"** · 41 · flight leader · `roo`
Born in Kohlgard, schooled in Kohlgard, flew for Verre for twenty years. Quiet, economical, drinks
on his own. He teaches the player the flight model in Act 1 in four lines and never repeats
himself. He knows every enemy ace by their turn.
**Wants:** to not be asked to shoot people he was at school with. He will be asked in Act 4.

**Aurie Petch** · 19 · `aurie`
Delighted by all of it. Treats the Fall as the best game ever invented and is not wrong. Talks
constantly. The reason Act 1 is fun.
**Wants:** to be famous — a name, a photograph, a squadron pennant.
**Dies at level 33**, not in combat: unhooking her harness to reach a crate that was going to fall
short. The mechanic kills her, not an enemy. Nobody is to blame and that is the point.

**Odile Sarn** · 34 · `odile`
The only genuinely great pilot on the field, and cold about it. Says perhaps forty words in three
acts, all of them correct.
**Wants:** enough money to buy her sister's passage out of the Sallow — which is exactly what the
Concord Line is selling. She works out what the panniers are before anyone else, and in Act 3 she
resigns her commission and takes Line escort pay. She comes back in Act 5, flying for them, and
helps anyway.

## 3.3 The aces — the Duel roster

Six. Each is a **voice**, a **want**, and a **way of flying** — the third is the part agent B turns
into AI parameters, so each entry states it in mechanical terms.

---

### D1 · Wilhelm Baumgart — "the Old Man" · `baumgart` · Act 1, level 18
Fifty-eight. Has been flying since before there was a corps to fly for. Courteous to the point of
irritation. He was Roo's schoolfriend, and taught him the stall turn.

- **Flies:** perfect energy conservation. Never chases, never dives below you, never spends
  altitude he cannot buy back. Sits high and slightly behind and waits for you to make the mistake.
  Disengages cleanly when he is losing and is not ashamed of it.
- **Beat:** he *lets you win*. He is the tutorial for how a duel feels, and the first ace teaches
  you the game's actual thesis: height is money.
- **Wants:** to be released. He is too old, everyone knows it, and asking is not done.
- **Ends:** Act 4, level 76. He and Roo are put in the air against each other on purpose.

### D2 · Ilsabet Kohl-Marren — "the Countess" · `countess` · Act 2, level 38
Forties. The Kohlgard aerodrome is her lawn; she flies a preposterous over-engined racing triplane
with two guns she never loads properly. She is *delighted* by the player.

- **Flies:** boom-and-zoom taken to absurdity. Enormous speed, no turn, no patience. She converts
  every fight into the vertical and forces overshoots. She will break off mid-fight to fly through
  something for fun.
- **Beat:** her duel is scored as a **race**, not a kill — she is also the pylon/time-trial mode's
  face. You beat her by out-flying her, not by shooting her.
- **Wants:** for the war to be interesting again. She is bored, and being bored in this war is an
  obscenity she has not noticed.

### D3 · Anselm Drach — "the Wire" · `drach` · Act 3, level 56 → final, level 98
Twenty-seven, a technician, not gentry, and the only pilot in Kohlgard who was not given his
aeroplane. Flat voice, over-modulated set, never raises it. **He is the villain and the one voice
in this game Kokoro cannot do** — see `SUNO.md` §3.2.

- **Flies:** low, flat and ugly. Ignores you entirely if there is a canopy in reach; he is hunting
  *crates*, and he shoots silk with a synchronised cannon at ranges nobody else attempts. Will take
  a hit to get a canopy. Extremely hard to bait into a turning fight, because he does not want one.
- **Beat:** level 56 is **a duel you survive rather than win** — he goes into the Deck and is gone.
  Level 98 is the last fight in the game.
- **Wants:** the estate he was not born into. Every canopy is a line in a ledger somebody senior
  will one day read.
- **The line that fixes him:** *"I have shot four hundred canopies. I have never once opened one."*

### D4 · Sabin Grelle — "the Bailiff" · `grelle` · Act 4, level 70
Concord Line's own escort ace. Black machine, no markings, no nationality, no rank. Appears
whenever a player threatens a drop, and only then.

- **Flies:** purely defensively. Positions between you and the airship, mirrors you, refuses to
  shoot first, and is almost impossible to get past. Fights to a stalemate on purpose, because a
  stalemate is a delivery completed.
- **Beat:** **a duel you cannot win by shooting.** You win level 70 by getting a crate, not by
  getting him. If the player empties their guns into him for four minutes they lose the level with
  him still flying, and that is the intended first outcome.
- **Wants:** nothing to change. He is the most dangerous want in the game.

### D5 · Yannik and Ott Ferber — "the Brothers" · `ferber_y` `ferber_o` · Act 4, level 67
Farm boys from an estate near Hallowmere. Fly as a pair and have never flown any other way. One
scout, one two-seater with an observer's gun.

- **Flies:** genuine two-ship tactics — one baits low and slow, the other holds height and comes
  down on whoever takes the bait. The only fight in the game where the correct answer is to refuse
  the first target.
- **Beat:** if you drive one off, the other stops fighting entirely and follows him. That is the
  intended, and slightly awful, win.
- **Wants:** to go home together. They are the reason the flight stops enjoying this.

### D6 · Katrin Sohl — "the Widow of Marnhault" · `—` · **The Long Patrol only**, after midnight
Unlockable. Flies a black-green machine with no lights. **She has no dialogue at all — not one
line, in any mode.** She never answers a hail. She is the only character in the game with no voice
key and no text card, and that costs nothing to build and is the best thing in the endless mode.

- **Flies:** the mirror. Her AI is a copy of the player's own last recorded flight profile from the
  previous three Long Patrol runs — same climb rate preference, same turn direction bias.
- **Wants:** unstated. Deliberately.

### D7 · Odile Sarn — the friendly duel · Act 5, level 87
Not an enemy. She is flying Line escort by then, and level 87 is a race between two people who used
to share a mess. Uses the Duel-mode frame with the guns disabled.

# 4. The five acts

Each act is **20 levels**. The table below is what agent B's 100-level design must reconcile
against: the **beat levels** are load-bearing and their numbers should be treated as fixed unless
the manager moves them; everything between them is agent B's to shape.

## Act 1 · levels 1–20 · **THE FALL**

**Theatre:** the west Sallow, the Belt and the Floor. Low, green, wet, spring.
**Situation:** ordinary crate war. It is a sport, it is fun, and the game teaches its whole flight
model here without ever saying the word "tutorial".
**The turn (L15):** a crate comes down in the flak with paperwork in it, and the paperwork is a
Concord invoice — billed to **both** fields for the **same** crate. The Fall is not charity. It is
a business with the squadron inside it.

| level | beat |
|---|---|
| **1** | **First Fall** — opener. One crate, no enemy, no guns fired. Climb, get under it, land. |
| **3** | **Post Route** — Nell's old mail run. Six of nine villages are under water. Establishes her. |
| **6** | **The Judge's Box** — hangar unlocks. Ferry's five-line introduction. First upgrade. |
| **9** | **Above The Deck** — first climb through cloud. Aurie sees the sun. Roo's "height is money". |
| **12** | **Two Fields** — first canopy shot. You learn you can *deny* a crate, and how that feels. |
| **15** | **Flak Belt** — **act turn.** The invoice. |
| **18** | **The Old Man** — **DUEL: Baumgart.** |
| **20** | **Ferrow Green, Evening** — act closer. No combat. Come home in the dark, land by lamplight. |

## Act 2 · levels 21–40 · **SUFFICIENCY**

**Theatre:** the Deck, and the Kettle shore. Cloud, water, mines, no forced landing anywhere.
**Situation:** the drops go onto a timetable — eleven and four, every day, weather or not. Verre
starts flying to the Line's clock. The fighting gets professional and stops being funny.
**The turn (L33):** Aurie dies reaching for a crate. Then (L36) Roo plots the drop log on the
squadron map and it is a perfect alternation: one for us, one for them, forever. Nobody is being
supplied. Both sides are being *sustained*.

| level | beat |
|---|---|
| **21** | **Sufficiency** — opener. The first timetabled drop; four aeroplanes waiting at eleven. |
| **24** | **Schedule** — Hurdy starts calling the drops before they happen. It is worse than useful. |
| **28** | **The Kettle Shore** — new theatre. Mined water below; no forced landing exists in this level. |
| **31** | **Race** — the Countess turns up and does not shoot at anybody. Pylon-mode story level. |
| **33** | **Harness** — **act turn. Aurie dies.** |
| **36** | **Weight** — heavy crates; the altitude/mass penalty is introduced. Roo plots the drop log. |
| **38** | **The Countess** — **DUEL: Kohl-Marren.** Scored as a race. |
| **40** | **Two Empty Chairs** — act closer. Hangar, rain, an aeroplane nobody is repairing. |

**Theatre:** over Kohlgard, and — worse — over Verrine towns.
**Situation:** the Air Board's new order: deny the crate wherever it falls, *including over our own

| level | beat |
|---|---|

### The choice at level 76

The only branch in the story. It changes tone, two lines of dialogue in Act 5, and the epilogue
card — **it does not change level content, unlock state, or difficulty.** Deliberately cheap.

| branch | what the player does | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Interfere** | Break the fight up. Put yourself between them. | Both survive. Roo is court-martialled off the flight and is a civilian in the Act 5 hangar, which he hates. Baumgart is quietly retired and writes once. |
| **Let it happen** | Hold off and watch. | Baumgart lets Roo win, as he has let everyone win for two years. Roo knows. He does not speak again until level 93. |

Both are correct. Neither is rewarded. The debrief for both is Hurdy saying nothing.

## Act 5 · levels 81–100 · **THE SUFFICIENCY**

**Theatre:** the Blue, the Lane, and finally the Mud again.
**Situation:** the war ended nineteen days ago and nobody stopped, because the Line's contracts run
to quarter-end and the treaty has no clause about freight. Everything after level 81 is being done
by two air arms that have already been demobilised on paper, over goods delivered under a contract
that both signatories are now at peace under.
**The turn (L90):** the flight stops trying to win the Fall and starts trying to end it. Odile —
now Line escort — hands over the last manifest.

| level | beat |
|---|---|
| **81** | **Nineteen Days** — opener. Hurdy reads the armistice date off a telegram, twice. |
| **84** | **Nobody Told The Sallow** — the war continuing out of pure contractual inertia. |
| **87** | **Odile Returns** — friendly duel/race with Sarn in Line black. |
| **90** | **The Last Manifest** — **act turn.** Twelve crates, no kills, no denial. Airlift's biggest level. |
| **93** | **The Blue** — the highest level in the game. The ceiling, the Kettle Sea, and the sun at once. |
| **96** | **The Sufficiency** — the flagship, over the Sallow, dropping the last quarter. |
| **98** | **Drach** — **DUEL. The last fight in the game.** |
| **100** | **The Post** — epilogue. No guns. One bag of mail. Marrender. Credits. |

### Level 100 is not a fight and must not become one

It is the closer, it is three minutes long, it has one crate in it, and the crate is a mail sack.
The whole level is landing on a strip of dyke at Marrender in front of about forty people. If
anything in the design wants a boss here, the boss is at 98.

# 5. How story reaches a player who is flying

Four contexts, and **nothing blocks**. No modal, no confirm, no full-screen "press to continue"
that eats a frame of flying. The brief is explicit and Aaron has said it in three other projects.

## 5.1 The four contexts

| ctx | where | when | budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| `brief` | the mission card on the map/hangar screen | before launch, already stopped | ≤ 3 lines |
| `radio` | a single card, top third in portrait, top-left in landscape | in flight, over the action | ≤ 6 per mission |
| `debrief` | over the results panel, which is already there | after landing | ≤ 2 lines |
| `hangar` | a small card in the corner of the upgrade screen | between missions, ignorable | 2–5 lines |

## 5.2 Line lengths, measured against the actual viewport

Portrait 9:19.5 gives roughly **390 CSS px** of usable width. At the HUD's 17 px face with card
padding that is **40–46 characters** on one line before it wraps.

| ctx | hard cap | why |
|---|---|---|
| `radio` | **44 characters, one line, never wraps** | it is read in a glance while the thumb is busy. A wrapped radio card is a design failure, not a long line. |
| `brief` situation | 90 chars (wraps to 2) | the player is stopped and can read. |
| `brief` objective | 60 chars | it must survive being re-read at a glance mid-mission from the pause card. |
| `debrief` | 70 chars per line, 2 lines | stopped, but nobody reads a results screen for long. |
| `hangar` | 80 chars per line | stopped, and skippable entirely. |

**Every line in §6 obeys these.** A `script_len` gate should assert it, because the first person to
write a 60-character radio line will not notice on a desktop browser.

## 5.3 Radio timing — the numbers

```
show_ms   = clamp(2600 + 55 * len(text), 2600, 5500)     # when there is no audio
show_ms   = audio_duration_ms + 900                       # when there is
gap_ms    = 700                                           # minimum silence between two cards
queue     = 2 deep, oldest dropped
```

**Suppression rules — a line must never fire while the player is busy:**

- not in the **first 4 s** or the **last 3 s** of a mission;
- not while a target is inside the gun cone (auto-fire is on, so this is "not while shooting");
- not below **150 m** (the Mud — the player is about to hit something);
- not within 2 s of a crate catch or a crate loss, which have their own one-word confirmations;
- **story beat lines outrank pooled chatter and cancel a queued pooled line outright.** Do not
  crossfade them. A story line that arrives after its moment is worse than a story line dropped.

**Six foreground radio lines per mission is the ceiling and four is the target.** Beat levels get
six; ordinary levels get three or four, drawn from pools. A mission with eight lines in it reads as
nagging within about ten minutes of play, and this game is a hundred missions long.

## 5.4 The pools — how the other ~72 levels sound

Only the 40 beat levels in §4 have written scenes. Everything else is **pooled chatter**: shuffle
bag per group, per-line cooldown, act-gated. Groups, with a target pool size:

| group | fires on | pool |
|---|---|---|
| `takeoff` | wheels up | 8 |
| `crate_spot` | a canopy enters view | 10 |
| `crate_caught` | catch | 12 |
| `crate_lost` | it lands on the wrong field | 10 |
| `canopy_shot` | you cut a silk | 8 |
| `enemy_in` | a hostile commits | 10 |
| `hit_taken` | real damage | 8 |
| `stall` | you fall out of a turn | 6 |
| `flak` | you enter the Belt | 6 |
| `zeppelin` | an airship is in the level | 6 |
| `low_fuel` | reserve | 4 |
| `rtb` | objective complete | 8 |
| `landed` | wheels down | 8 |
| `blooded` | you shoot a pilot in his parachute | 7 · §6.7 |

**The rule that will otherwise ship broken:** a dead or departed speaker's lines are **removed from
every bag at the act boundary that kills them**, and any line of theirs already sitting in the
queue is **dropped, not played**. Aurie is dead from level 34; Odile is gone from 54 and back at
87 with a *different* pool (Line phrasing, not squadron phrasing). A cheerful bark from Aurie at
level 61 is the single worst bug this system can have and it is one line of code to prevent.

## 5.5 What a brief card looks like

```
        ACT ONE · MISSION 15
        FLAK BELT

        hundred metres. That was on purpose.

        HURDY  "Nine hundred. Through the flak. Lovely."

Title, situation, objective, and **at most one** character line. The card is dismissed by launching;

## 6.6 The two enemy takes in full

These two are written as continuous takes because they are `SUNO.md` §3.2's SUNO generations. Each
row is one **take line**, and each take line becomes **one or two cards** in the game — the rows

"text": ["I have cut four hundred canopies.", "I have never once opened one."]
```

generate two clips — Kokoro and SUNO both put the beat in the performance and cutting between two
files loses it.

# 7. The audio-optional contract

**The rule:** the game is fully playable, fully legible, and fully *paced* with `assets/audio/`
empty or deleted. There is no line the player can only hear and no line the player can only read.

## 7.1 One source of truth

Every line in §6 lives once, in `data/script.json`, and the audio manifest is **generated from it**.
Nobody hand-writes a manifest and nobody hand-writes a text card.

```json
{
  "id":    "a1_l15_hurdy_04",
  "act":   1,
  "level": 15,
  "ctx":   "radio",
  "who":   "hurdy",
  "text":  "Ours and theirs. Same crate. Same number.",
  "dir":   "reading it twice because she does not believe it",
  "audio": "vo/hurdy/a1_l15_hurdy_04.mp3",
  "dur":   3.1
}
```

| field | required | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | **yes** | `a<act>_l<lvl>_<who>_<nn>` for story, `<group>_<nn>` for pooled, `<who>_<nn>` for ace takes |
| `text` | **yes, non-empty** | what is shown. Not a subtitle of the audio — the audio is a performance of *it*. May be a **string**, or an **array** of strings when one spoken line needs two cards (§6.6); every element obeys §5.2's cap and V1 checks each one |
| `who` | **yes** | key into the cast table; supplies the on-card speaker label and the voice id |
| `ctx` | **yes** | one of `brief` `radio` `debrief` `hangar` |
| `dir` | no | for generation and for a human reader. Never rendered |
| `audio` | **no** | advisory. Absent, missing on disk, or failed to decode are all the same case |
| `dur` | no | seconds, written by the generator from the actual file. Absent → §5.3's text formula |

## 7.2 The loader's rule, in one sentence

**A line's card is scheduled from `text`; audio, if it happens to be there, is played alongside it
and only lengthens it.**

```
if (audio decoded)  show_ms = dur*1000 + 900
else                show_ms = clamp(2600 + 55*text.length, 2600, 5500)
```

Never the other way round. If the card duration is derived from the audio and the audio is missing,
the silent build gets a 0 ms card, which is exactly how "playable with the folder empty" ships
broken while passing a boot test.

## 7.3 The four gates

`tools/gates_vo.mjs`. Each must be shown to be able to fail (`--falsify`).

| | gate | what it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | every entry has a non-empty `text`, and every string in it is ≤ its ctx's cap in §5.2 | no audio-only line can exist; no radio card wraps. Run it against §6 of this file as well as against `script.json`, so the doc and the data cannot drift |
| **V2** | every `.mp3` under `assets/audio/vo/` is named by an id present in `script.json` | **an orphan file is a failure.** This is the gate that catches a line that was generated and then cut from the script, and it is the one people skip |
| **V3** | boot and play three beat levels with `assets/audio/` renamed away; assert every scheduled card appeared with a non-zero duration and the mission completed | the empty-folder contract, measured rather than asserted |
| **V4** | every `who` in `script.json` exists in the cast table and has a voice id in `SUNO.md` §3 | a typo'd speaker key renders a blank label, which reads as a bug and never crashes |

**V3 is the one that matters and it must move the files, not set a flag.** A `?noaudio=1` path that
skips the audio layer proves that the *flag* works. Renaming the directory proves the *game* works.
This repo has been burned by exactly that distinction more than once.

## 7.4 Speaker labels

Rendered from `who`, never from the line. The card shows `HURDY  "line"` — the label is the short
name in §3, uppercased, and it is the same string in text-only and audio builds. A player who has
never heard a voice must still be able to tell Roo from Ferry after ten missions, so the labels
carry the whole load in a silent build and they are not optional.

## 7.5 What is deliberately not covered

Sound *effects* — engine, guns, wind, flak, the silk snapping — are not in this contract. They are
synthesised or sampled by agent A's audio layer and have nothing to do with the script. The
contract here is about **lines**, because a line is the only kind of audio whose absence would
otherwise cost the player information.
